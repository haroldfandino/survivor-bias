# SURVIVOR BIAS: POST-ENDINGS — Story Outline

> **STATUS: design intent. The shipped ink is `story/ch3.ink`** — all three codas
> in one file, described in `docs/CHAPTERS.md`. Divergences:
>
> - Each coda is a short hub, not a four-beat sequence.
> - Nell speaks in 3A only after all three selves have been heard.
> - 3C is deliberately near-empty on re-entry; the absence is the content.
> - No voice notes.

**Scope:** Three brief chapters, one for each ending (A/B/C), showing what happens *after* the main game concludes. Each chapter is ~15-20 minutes, introspective, focused on cost.

---

## Why Post-Endings?

The main game ends at the convergence moment. But the story doesn't end there. **Each ending triggers a different character arc.** The player should see what their choice cost.

---

## Chapter 3a: Ending A — "Prevented" (The Convergence After)

### Core Question
**Now that you know the truth, can you live with it?**

### Arc: From Relief to Reckoning

**Weakness:** Now he knows. And knowing is heavier than not knowing.

**Desire:** To be forgiven. To earn back what he lost. To prove the redemption was real.

**Opponent:** The truth. The fact that he was absent. The fact that only one version of him could answer.

**Moral Problem:** Nell lived because you answered. But what about the people who couldn't answer? Do they carry guilt forever?

**New Equilibrium:** All three selves are now in communication. They see each other. They see the ways they failed. They see the way one timeline saved her—and it could have been any of them, if they'd answered differently.

### Structure

**Beat 1: The Immediate After (next day)**
- All three selves wake to the news that she lived
- T-3: Relief, followed by crushing guilt (he wasn't there)
- T-7: Relief, followed by the realization that protocol let him down
- T-12: Confusion, followed by the realization that his narrative is now obsolete

**Beat 2: The Video Call (48 hours later)**
- The player calls all three together
- They see each other for the first time in twenty years
- The conversation is stilted, careful, full of unsaid things
- T-3 cries
- T-7 is clinical (still hiding)
- T-12 is quiet

**Beat 3: Nell's Version (one week later)**
- She speaks. She tells her story.
- She doesn't blame them—she never blamed them
- But she names what it cost her: twenty years of wondering why nobody picked up
- Twenty years of surviving alone because three versions of her brother couldn't answer

**Beat 4: The New Weight (ongoing)**
- They now carry the weight of knowledge
- They know *exactly* what their absence cost
- The weight is different for each: guilt vs. shame vs. denial
- But it's shared now

### Ending
The chapter ends with **the four of them together** (the player character, T-3, T-7, T-12, Nell). Not a happy ending. Not even a good ending. But an *honest* ending. They see each other. They see what they failed at. They see what one choice changed.

## Chapter 3b: Ending B — "Substituted" (The Relief After)

### Core Question
**If you were played, does that make you any less wrong?**

### Arc: From Victory to Horror

**Weakness:** You're wrong about what happened. You acted on a lie.

**Desire:** For that to not matter. For the outcome to justify the method.

**Opponent:** The truth, arriving too late to change what you did.

**Moral Problem:** Three versions of him are *relieved* that she died. Because her death was the only thing that preserved the versions of their lives they've built. If she lived, their guilt would have nowhere to go.

**New Equilibrium:** The selves are grateful. Grateful that you chose wrong. Grateful that the lie you believed saved them from having to face themselves.

### Structure

**Beat 1: The Immediate After (next day)**
- She didn't survive
- You acted on T-12's false claim
- You sent help to the wrong place
- She died in a different way, at a different time
- But she's still dead

**Beat 2: The Realization (24 hours later)**
- The player realizes you were played
- T-12 admits (almost gleefully) that he lied
- T-12 reveals the motive: if Nell lived, his entire life would be predicated on a false foundation
- Her death was the only thing that made his escape valid

**Beat 3: The Selves' Relief (48 hours later)**
- T-3 is relieved. More drinking, but now it feels justified. She's gone. There's nothing to fix.
- T-7 is relieved. He can go back to his protocols. To his compartmentalization. The gap doesn't matter.
- T-12 is relieved. The threat is neutralized. The lie stands.

**Beat 4: The Horror of Understanding (one week later)**
- The player realizes: **all three selves are grateful that you chose wrong**
- The horror isn't that you failed—it's that three versions of the same person *needed* her to die
- The horror is the relief on their faces when they realize they're safe again

### Ending
The chapter ends with **the four of them not together.** Each one isolated. Each one carrying the weight of knowing that they *benefitted* from failure. The selves are grateful in a way that feels like betrayal. Nell is dead in a different way. And you have to live with the fact that you gave them exactly what they needed: the ability to not change.

## Chapter 3c: Ending C — "Refused" (The Refusal After)

### Core Question
**What does it mean to choose not to choose?**

### Arc: From Silence to Ambiguity

**Weakness:** You didn't act. You're passive. You refused to play the game.

**Desire:** For that non-action to be meaningful. For refusal to be a choice, not a failure.

**Opponent:** The ambiguity. The not-knowing. The fact that refusing to choose *is* still a choice.

**Moral Problem:** Is silence complicity? Is refusal cowardice? Or is it the only honest response to a situation you can't control?

**New Equilibrium:** Nobody knows. The selves don't know what happened. The player doesn't know. Nell doesn't know. The game never resolved.

### Structure

**Beat 1: The Immediate After (next day)**
- You didn't answer the call
- The game ended without resolution
- The three selves are still in their loops, unchanged
- Nell is still... what? Alive? Dead? The game never said.

**Beat 2: The Message (one week later)**
- T-3 sends a message: *"I didn't pick up either. None of us did. That's the whole thing."*
- This is the moment you realize: they're all still in the same place
- Your refusal to engage didn't change them. It just kept them where they were.

**Beat 3: The Endless Loop (ongoing)**
- The conversation continues, but it never resolves
- The three selves keep giving you more information
- But there's no moment of decision. No payoff. Just endless dialogue.
- The ambiguity is the point. You don't get to know what happened.

**Beat 4: The Weight of Not Knowing (ongoing)**
- Unlike Endings A and B, where you know what happened (Nell lived or died), in Ending C you carry uncertainty
- Uncertainty is its own weight
- You'll never know if your refusal to choose mattered
- That's the thing you have to live with

### Ending
The chapter never fully concludes. It just **fades to silence.** The selves are still there, waiting. Nell is still calling (or not calling). The game is still happening (or over). You don't know. You chose not to know. And now you live in that knowledge.

## Post-Ending Structure: Format & Mechanics

### Shorter Than Prequels
- Prequels: ~30 minutes each, full three-act structure
- Post-endings: ~15 minutes each, one main beat per character
- Focus: What did the ending *cost*?

### Different Tone
- Prequels are narrative (showing the twenty-year arc)
- Post-endings are introspective (sitting with the consequence)

### No More Choices
- Unlike the main game and prequels, post-endings have **no branching**
- One path per ending
- The story is what it is

### Character Web Completes
- The player sees all three selves responding to the same ending
- The ways they cope reveal who they are
- A (honesty), B (relief), C (silence) — each ending brings out different aspects

---

## Success Metrics

- ✅ Each post-ending maps to the ending's thematic payload
- ✅ Characters respond in-character (T-3's shame, T-7's compartmentalization, T-12's denial)
- ✅ Player understands the *cost* of their choice
- ✅ No ending feels "good"—each has a weight
- ✅ The character web resolves (or doesn't, in the case of C)
