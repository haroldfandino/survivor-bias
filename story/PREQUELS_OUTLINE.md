**Scope:** Three prequel chapters, one per timeline, covering the 20 years from Nell's death → Tonight. Each converges on **01:38 — the moment she called.**

---

## Context: Why Prequels?

The main game (Tonight) is a **mystery about a specific moment in the past.** The prequels answer: *How did each self get here?* Each timeline is a different accommodation with the same guilt.

**Unifying principle:** All three prequels happen across 20 years but **climax at the exact same moment: 01:38, when Nell calls.** In the main game, you are awake on your phone. In the prequels, you see what each self was doing when that call came through — and whether they answered.

## Chapter 2a: Timeline-3 — "The Stayed"

### Core Arc
**A man drinks his way into a corner, then runs out of room to hide.**

### 22-Block Structure

| Block # | Block Name | Application | Implementation |
|---------|-----------|---|---|
| 1 | **Weakness** | He lacks impulse control. Drinks to escape. Avoids consequence. | Opening: He's in a bar 3 years after Nell's death. Still drinks, but pretends it's social. |
| 2 | **Desire** | He wants to be forgiven (by Nell, by the town, by himself). | Tries to be useful. Volunteers. Helps friends. Uses action as penance. |
| 3 | **Opponent** | Time. His own weakness. The fact that she's gone. | Every attempt at redemption is undercut by another drink. 20 years of this cycle. |
| 4 | **Ally** | The town. His friends. His own memory of who he was. | People keep giving him chances. He keeps taking them for granted. |
| 5 | **Incompleteness** | He doesn't remember that night clearly. Pieces are missing. | Unreliable narrator. What he remembers shifts depending on how much he's drunk. |
| 6 | **Moral Problem** | Is it enough to feel sorry? Can regret substitute for change? | He apologizes constantly. But he doesn't stop drinking. |
| 7 | **Battle Plan** | He decides (yet again) to get sober. Makes a vow. | Joins a program. Goes to meetings. Tells people he's turning it around. |
| 8 | **Battle** | He stays sober for a while. Weeks. Months. Gets 6 months sober, buys a chip. | Then something triggers him. A date. An anniversary. A bad day. He relapses. |
| 9 | **Self-Revelation** | He realizes he's addicted not to alcohol but to punishment. | The drinking is the sentence he's serving to himself. Sobriety means facing what he did. |
| 10 | **Audience Revelation** | He's the one with the story, not the facts. We see his unreliability. | Memory is shaped by guilt and alcohol. He's convinced he's the worst, but is he? |
| 11 | **New Equilibrium** | 01:38 — The phone rings. Nell calls. He's in a bar. Drunk. Phone goes to voicemail. | He misses the call. The culminating failure. Or: He answers. Slurred. She hangs up. Either way: he was not available. |
| 12-22 | **Secondary arcs** | His relationship with T-7 (judgment), T-12 (absent), his family (disappointed). | The people watching him fail, repeatedly. The cost to others of his self-destruction. |

### Character Arc: 20-Year Descent with Moments of Hope

**Act I: The First Years (Post-Death)**
- **Setting:** Small town, 3 years after Nell's death
- **Beat 1:** He's at a pub with work mates. Seems normal. Laughing. But he's on his 4th drink and the night is young. Someone mentions Nell casually — *"How's your family doing?"* — and he goes quiet.
- **Beat 2:** He leaves early, drives home slightly unsteady. The town is small. Everyone knows what happened. He feels their eyes.
- **Beat 3:** At home, he finds her old room. Untouched. He closes the door quickly. He pours another drink. Faster this time.
- **Inciting Incident:** He makes a vow to himself: *"I'm going to be better. For her."* He means it. He really does.

**Act II: The Cycle (7 Years Later)**
- **Setting:** Same town, 10 years after Nell's death
- **Beat 1:** He's been sober for 8 months. Going to meetings. People are impressed. He feels like he's paid enough.
- **Beat 2:** He sees T-7 in town (now a paramedic). T-7 nods but doesn't stop to talk. That small rejection lands hard.
- **Beat 3:** That night, he has one drink. Just one. Just to take the edge off. 
- **Beat 4:** Two weeks later, he's back to drinking every night. But he tells himself it's not as bad as before. It's controlled.
- **Turning Point:** A moment of clarity in a dark bar at 2 AM. He realizes he's been making the same promise for 10 years. It's not working. He cries. Alone in a booth.
- **Response:** He calls T-7. Wants to apologize. T-7 is polite but distant. *"We all did what we could."* The distance kills him more than anger would have.

**Act III: The Reckoning (17 Years Later)**
- **Setting:** Same town, 20 years after Nell's death (present day)
- **Beat 1:** He's still in the same patterns. Sober stretches, relapses, shame spirals. His body is wearing. His hands shake. His memory is truly unreliable now (not just from guilt, but from years of drinking).
- **Beat 2:** He gets a message from T-12, forwarded from the player. *"She's calling tonight. 01:38."* The specificity terrifies him.
- **Beat 3:** He goes to a bar to settle his nerves. (Old habit. Still old habit.) By 01:38, he's had 5 drinks. The phone rings. He doesn't recognize the number. He's too drunk to answer. It goes to voicemail.
- **Climax:** Later, he finds out what that call was. His sister, asking him to come get her. He could have saved her. He was 15 minutes away. Instead, he was drunk in a bar, unable to answer.
- **New Equilibrium:** In the conversation with the player (main game), he is honest about this. He was drunk. He missed the call. He's been drinking to forget ever since. The player hears the weight of this. Either redemption or resignation — the player will choose how he responds.

### Key Mechanics: Memory as Unreliability

T-3's entire arc is about **how unreliable memory becomes under the weight of guilt and substance abuse.**

- Early in the prequel, he remembers Nell's death with detail: *"She was wearing that blue jacket. The one with the rip in the pocket."*
- Later, he misremembers: *"The jacket was black, I think. Or maybe green?"*
- By the end, he doesn't trust his own memories at all: *"I don't know what I remember about that night anymore. It's all mixed up."*

This sets up the main game perfectly: **T-3 is emotionally honest but factually unreliable.** His weakness is that he knows it, and it eats at him.

### Ending (Cliff-Hanger into Tonight)

The prequel ends at 01:38 with the call going unanswered. The player knows what T-3 doesn't: **This is not the past. This is happening right now. In the main game, you will have a choice about that call.**

## Chapter 2b: Timeline-7 — "The Atoned"

### Core Arc
**A man chooses purpose as a shield, then realizes he's defending against the wrong enemy.**

### 22-Block Structure

| Block # | Block Name | Application | Implementation |
|---------|-----------|---|---|
| 1 | **Weakness** | He avoids accountability through precision and service. | Opening: He's a student, 3 years post-Nell, applying to paramedic training. |
| 2 | **Desire** | He wants to be useful. To save people. To prove he can't fail again. | Becomes a paramedic. Good one. Careful. By-the-book. Saves lives. |
| 3 | **Opponent** | Himself. The gap in his memory (01:40-01:55, where was he?). Guilt disguised as duty. | Every life he saves is proof he's not that person anymore. But what if he is? |
| 4 | **Ally** | His job. The protocols. The people he saves. His colleagues who trust him. | The paramedic service becomes his identity. He is *useful*. That's enough. |
| 5 | **Incompleteness** | There's a 15-minute gap that night. He won't explain it. He can't. | The gap is his secret. He's built his entire life on the lie that he was nowhere near her. |
| 6 | **Moral Problem** | Is atonement real if it's built on a lie? Can you be good while hiding evil? | Every life he saves, he tells himself he's balancing the scales. But the scales are rigged. |
| 7 | **Battle Plan** | He decides the best atonement is to be so good, so skilled, so useful that he earns forgiveness. | 17 years. Thousands of calls. Commendations. Respect. He's the best paramedic in three counties. |
| 8 | **Battle** | He performs his role perfectly. But performance is hollow. He knows the gap. He's still hiding. | A call comes in — a young woman, similar age to Nell. He saves her. He feels relief, but not absolution. |
| 9 | **Self-Revelation** | He realizes: *His job is not about saving her. It's about forgetting her.* | The paramedic isn't penance. It's a distraction. He can't save Nell; he can only save proxies. |
| 10 | **Audience Revelation** | We see the cost of his compartmentalization. He's precise about everything except himself. | His emotional life is as closed-off as his paramedic protocols are open. |
| 11 | **New Equilibrium** | 01:38 — The phone rings. Nell calls. He's at a call. He doesn't answer. Protocol: finish the call first. By the time he's free, it's too late. | He was *there*, at the gate, exactly where he was that night 20 years ago. He's been there the whole time, unable to move. |
| 12-22 | **Secondary arcs** | His relationship with T-3 (disappointment in his weakness), his colleagues (don't know his secret), his internal dialogue (constant justification). | The people he could tell but doesn't. The relief that never comes. |

### Character Arc: Professional Armor Over Personal Void

**Act I: The Flight into Purpose (Post-Death)**
- **Setting:** Small town hospital, 3 years after Nell's death
- **Beat 1:** He's visiting the hospital, ostensibly for a class project. He wanders into the ER. Watches the organized chaos. A paramedic team brings in a patient, quick, competent, life-saving.
- **Beat 2:** He's transfixed. This is what he wants. This is what he *needs*. Purpose. Measurable impact. Clear outcomes (save or don't save — no gray area).
- **Beat 3:** He applies to paramedic training that week. His parents are surprised but supportive. He tells them: *"I want to help people."* He means it, sort of.
- **Inciting Incident:** During training, he's taught protocols for crisis management. Everything has a procedure. Everything has a right way to do it. He thinks: *"If I follow the right way, I can't fail."*

**Act II: The Competence Trap (10 Years Later)**
- **Setting:** Ambulance station, 13 years after Nell's death
- **Beat 1:** He's been a paramedic for 10 years. He's excellent. Known for efficiency, care, and composure under pressure.
- **Beat 2:** A call comes in for a young woman, age 19, found collapsed. Similar build to Nell. Similar clothes. He runs the call. Gets her to the hospital. She survives. 
- **Beat 3:** After his shift, he finds himself outside her hospital room. She wakes up. She thanks him. He feels... empty. This should have felt like redemption. Instead, it feels like a performance.
- **Beat 4:** He goes home and writes in a journal (first time in years): *"If I save 1000 people, does that make up for the one I couldn't save?"* He tears out the page immediately.
- **Turning Point:** He realizes that every save is a proxy for Nell. He's trying to retroactively change that night by being good enough now. But she's still dead. No amount of future goodness changes that.
- **Response:** He doesn't tell anyone. He just becomes even more meticulous. If anything, more professional. More distant.

**Act III: The Unraveling (18 Years Later)**
- **Setting:** Same ambulance station, 20 years after Nell's death
- **Beat 1:** He's respected, promoted, seen as a mentor. Young paramedics admire his precision. He teaches classes on crisis response. On paper, he's fine.
- **Beat 2:** Off-shift, he's not fine. He has nightmares about that night. About the gap (01:40-01:55). Where was he? He knows. He was at the gate. Waiting. Hoping. Failing.
- **Beat 3:** The player contacts all three timelines. The message is specific: *"Nell's calling at 01:38 tonight."* 
- **Beat 4:** He goes to work that day, trying to be normal. But he can't focus. He keeps checking the time. By 01:37, he's on a call across town. The radio crackles.
- **Climax:** At 01:38, his phone buzzes. A text? A call? He can't check — mid-call, protocol is protocol. By the time he's free, 3 minutes later, it's too late. He missed her. Again.
- **New Equilibrium:** In the main game, he tells the player: *"I know what can be undone inside fifteen minutes."* Because he's been unwinding the same fifteen minutes (his original gap) for twenty years, and he's no closer to changing it.

### Key Mechanic: The Gap (01:40-01:55)

T-7's entire prequel hinges on **one thing he won't explain: where he was for fifteen minutes that night.** The player will discover, through cross-examination in the main game, that he was at the ford, at the gate, exactly where Nell would walk hours later. He was waiting for someone (not her). She wasn't his responsibility. But he was there.

- In the prequel, we see him at the gate that night, in flashback. Young. Hopeful. Then Nell never comes. He goes home. Later, he finds out she went the long way, through the water. He wasn't there to see it.
- In the present (main game), he's still at the gate, metaphorically. Waiting. Unable to move. Unable to forgive himself for being in the wrong place at a specific time.

### Ending (Cliff-Hanger into Tonight)

The prequel ends with him missing the call at 01:38, unable to answer because of protocol, because of duty, because of the paramedic he's become. The irony: **He became a paramedic to save lives, but he can't save the one call that matters.**

## Chapter 2c: Timeline-12 — "Got Out"

### Core Arc
**A man builds a life on a lie, then can't afford to let it collapse.**

### 22-Block Structure

| Block # | Block Name | Application | Implementation |
|---------|-----------|---|---|
| 1 | **Weakness** | He's a coward. Leaves under pressure. Runs from consequence. | Opening: He leaves town immediately after Nell's death, within weeks. Doesn't face anyone. |
| 2 | **Desire** | He wants a new life. Clean. Far away. Built on his own terms, not Nell's death. | Builds a life in another city. Job, apartment, friends who don't know his past. |
| 3 | **Opponent** | The past. The truth. His own conscience (buried, but still there). Reality. | Any connection back to that night is a threat. He rewrites the narrative to protect it. |
| 4 | **Ally** | Distance. Time. The compartmentalization he's built. New people who don't know. | By 20 years later, he's almost convinced himself it doesn't matter. That he's fine. |
| 5 | **Incompleteness** | He doesn't know the full story of that night. He left before it happened. He's ignorant, not innocent. | He's built theories about what happened, all of them that reduce his culpability. |
| 6 | **Moral Problem** | Is a life built on a lie still a good life? Has he escaped consequence or just delayed it? | He's successful, happy, secure. But it's all built on sand. One truth collapses it all. |
| 7 | **Battle Plan** | He doesn't try to change or atone. He just *leaves*. Stays away. Constructs a narrative. | For 20 years, he's maintained the fiction: *"That wasn't my fault. I did what I had to do."* |
| 8 | **Battle** | He lives this life. Succeeds by all external measures. Makes money, has partners, builds things. | The battle is internal and invisible. Beneath the surface, he's terrified of being found out. |
| 9 | **Self-Revelation** | He realizes: *He can't afford to be wrong about the last 20 years.* If he admits guilt, he loses everything. | Not just the life he built, but the identity he wrapped around it. He IS a successful person. That must be true. |
| 10 | **Audience Revelation** | We see his manipulation and confidence as masking profound fragility. | His smooth exterior hides desperate self-preservation. He's sabotaging the player to protect his narrative. |
| 11 | **New Equilibrium** | 01:38 — The phone rings. Nell calls. He's 300 miles away, asleep. Phone on silent (old habit). He doesn't know she called. | He wakes up to a message from the player: *"Why did you lie about the ford?"* He panics. The narrative is collapsing. |
| 12-22 | **Secondary arcs** | His relationship with his partners (they don't know him), his colleagues (respect a version of him), his own buried shame. | The cost of the lie is his authenticity. He's performed so well he's forgotten who he was. |

### Character Arc: Success as Elaboration

**Act I: The Escape (Immediate Post-Death)**
- **Setting:** Train station, the day after Nell's funeral (actually the day before, he left so fast)
- **Beat 1:** He's packing a bag. His parents ask where he's going. He says: *"I need to get away for a while."* He doesn't come back.
- **Beat 2:** On the train, he has hours to think. He practices a story in his head: *"I wasn't involved. I didn't know what was going to happen. I couldn't have stopped it."* By the time he arrives at his destination, he believes it.
- **Beat 3:** He finds a room, a job, a new set of people. He tells them his name, his background (edited), his ambitions (real). He doesn't mention a sister.
- **Inciting Incident:** He meets someone at work who asks about his family. He says: *"I'm not in touch."* It sounds casual. It sounds fine. But it's his first conscious lie, the first plank in the structure he's building.

**Act II: The Construction (10 Years Later)**
- **Setting:** Apartment, city far from his origin, 13 years after Nell's death
- **Beat 1:** He's successful. Makes good money. Has a partner who loves him, friends who respect him, a career he's built from nothing.
- **Beat 2:** Someone from his hometown sees him online, sends a message: *"I remember you. What happened to your sister?"* He deletes the message. Blocks the account. Doesn't tell his partner why.
- **Beat 3:** His partner notices he never talks about his past. Asks gently. He says: *"Just a normal childhood. Nothing interesting."* The lie is smooth. Practiced. Boring.
- **Beat 4:** He tells himself: *"That person is gone. I'm not him anymore."* It's partially true. Mostly lie.
- **Turning Point:** A news article surfaces about a historical cold case (unrelated to Nell, but it triggers him). He realizes: **Lies are fragile. One piece of evidence, one person who remembers, and everything collapses.**
- **Response:** He doubles down. Moves to a different city (job relocation, perfect cover). Cuts off the last few connections to his hometown. Becomes even more guarded.

**Act III: The Reckoning (19 Years Later)**
- **Setting:** Office, new city, 20 years after Nell's death
- **Beat 1:** He's thriving. Recently promoted. The life is real and constructed simultaneously.
- **Beat 2:** The player's message arrives: *"We need to talk about that night."* His hands shake. This is what he's been running from.
- **Beat 3:** He considers not responding. But ignoring it makes him look guilty (guilty of what?). Instead, he crafts a response. Smooth. Confident. Plausible but false.
- **Beat 4:** In the main game, he gives the player information that's *almost* true. Close enough to check out. But subtly wrong in ways that protect him.
- **Climax:** The player interviews him alongside T-3 and T-7. He realizes they might know more than him (he left too early). Or they might know less. He can't risk it. He doubles down on his false claims, confident they're unprovable.
- **New Equilibrium:** If the player believes him, his narrative stands (ending B — relief). If the player disbelieves him, his lie unravels (ending A — exposed). Either way, he's been revealed as someone willing to sabotage others to protect himself.

### Key Mechanic: The Lie That Works

T-12's entire arc is about **how a well-constructed lie can be more plausible than the truth.** His false claims in the main game are:
- Specific enough to sound credible
- Checkable enough to seem confident
- Inconsistent with T-3 and T-7 in ways that make them look unreliable instead of him

By the time the player realizes T-12 is lying, they've already believed him half the time. That's the point: **T-12 is the most dangerous because he's the most confident.**

- In the prequel, we see him building the lie. The first lie was easy. The second was easier. By the 100th, it's automatic.
- In the present (main game), his lies are so embedded in his narrative that he might believe them himself.

### Ending (Cliff-Hanger into Tonight)

The prequel ends with him asleep 300 miles away while Nell calls. He doesn't know she called. He's safe in his ignorance. But the moment the player contacts him, that safety evaporates. Now he has to *choose* to lie, consciously, knowing someone is asking questions.

## Connecting Tissue: 01:38 as the Climax

All three prequels converge on a single moment: **01:38, when Nell calls.**

### In Each Timeline:

- **T-3:** In a bar, drunk, the phone rings. He doesn't answer. He's already unreliable, and this confirms it. He's simply not available.
- **T-7:** On a call across town, professional duty prevents him from checking his phone. He's compartmentalized so well that he doesn't realize this is the moment that matters. By the time he's free, she's gone.
- **T-12:** Asleep 300 miles away, phone on silent. He doesn't answer because he doesn't know to answer. He's so far away, literally and morally, that he's not even in the game at the moment it matters.

### Why This Moment?

In the main game, **you are the one on the phone at 01:38.** You are awake. Your phone is in your hand. You've been on it all night talking to three versions of yourself. When the call comes in, you have a choice: **answer or refuse.**

The prequels show that **every other version of you failed at this moment.** None of them were there. None of them could answer. In your timeline (tonight), you are the first version who *can.*

The convergence on 01:38 makes this moment sacred. **One answered call. The difference between four lives.**

## Character Web: How They Change Each Other (Across Time)

### The Three Selves Before and After

**Before Nell's Death (assumed context, not shown in prequels):**
- All three are versions of the same person
- All three are present and available (or were supposed to be)
- All three failed in slightly different ways

**After Nell's Death (prequels show this):**
- T-3 stays and drowns in guilt → drinking
- T-7 stays and escapes guilt through purpose → paramedic
- T-12 leaves and builds a wall around guilt → new life

**By Tonight (main game):**
- T-3's unreliability is visible (he admits it)
- T-7's compartmentalization is hidden (he performs competence)
- T-12's culpability is denied (he performs confidence)

The player's job, across all three games, is to **make them see each other honestly and see themselves as one person with three coping mechanisms.**

## Prequel Structure: Format & Mechanics

### Format: Same Chat-Based Interaction

Like the main game, prequels are **dialogue-driven, no branching puzzles.** The player is in conversation with one self across one 20-year arc, viewing key moments in real-time (or quasi-real-time).

### Mechanics: Timeline Jumps

Instead of three simultaneous conversations (like Tonight), each prequel is a **conversation with jumps in time.**

**Example structure for Chapter 2a (T-3):**
- Player message: *"Tell me about after she died."*
- T-3's response unfolds as a series of scenes/beats
- Each beat is a moment in time (3 years later, 10 years later, 20 years later)
- Player can ask about specific moments or jump forward
- Climax: 01:38. The phone rings. What does he do?

### Ink Implementation

Similar to the main game, but with **temporal markers instead of location markers.**

```ink
~ TIMELINE = "t3"
~ TIME_JUMP = 3  // 3 years post-Nell

-> conversation_start

= conversation_start
-> t3_opening

= t3_opening
"He's in a bar. It's the kind of place he's been going to for three years — same stool, same bartender, same lie he tells himself: 'Just one.'..."

+ [Ask him why he came here.]
    -> respond_to_motive

+ [Ask him about that night.]
    -> respond_to_memory

= respond_to_motive
"..."
~ TIME_JUMP = 7  // Jump to 7 years later
-> t3_cycle

= respond_to_memory
"He's quiet for a long time..."
~ TIME_JUMP = 10
-> t3_reckoning

// ... and so on until 01:38
```
